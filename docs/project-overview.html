Project overview · TUP College of Science

SIT Process Flow

A one-page guide for Computer Studies students doing their Supervised Industrial Training (SIT): what to do before, during and after training, the forms for each step, and the list of partner companies.

Static site, Firebase-hosted data (PaaS)Information portal with a small admin editorActive

1. What the app is for

Students used to ask the coordinator for the same steps and form links again and again. This page puts the whole SIT process in order, with each form's link beside the step that needs it, and a searchable list of Host Training Establishments (HTEs) that already have an MOA with TUP.

Students only read; they need no account. SIT staff sign in as admin to change the form links and to add, edit or remove partner companies. Admin sign-in logs out after 15 idle minutes, with a 30-second warning first.

Purpose

To guide students through the SIT requirements in order, so documents reach the right signatories and every trainee finishes with a complete final output.

Scope

BSCS, BSIS and BSIT students of the Computer Studies Department, from submitting the application documents to handing in the final output after 486 training hours.

References

  • The SIT forms in the department's Forms repository (linked on the page)
  • ISO 9001:2015, clause 7.5 Documented information
  • Republic Act No. 9470 (National Archives of the Philippines Act of 2007) for record retention

Responsibilities

StudentCompletes and submits the documents, applies to the company, fills the forms on time, renders 486 hours, submits the final output.
SIT CoordinatorChecks documents, validates a company the student chose, issues recommendation and endorsement letters, handles MOA signing, receives the final output, keeps the page's links and partner list current.
Department Head, IRJP DirectorSign the application documents.
Company (HTE)Accepts the trainee, gives the training plan, signs the MOA and training agreement, evaluates the trainee, issues the certificate of completion.

5. Architecture

  • Static page (HTML, CSS, plain JavaScript): all screens; no build step, served as files.
  • Firebase Authentication: admin sign-in only; students never sign in.
  • Cloud Firestore: the partner list and the form links. Who may write is decided by Firestore security rules in the Firebase console, not by the page.
  • Google Drive and Google Forms: hold the forms themselves; the page only links to them.
